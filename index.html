The idea

Speaking improves by speaking, out loud, every day

At B2 you already understand most things. What holds speaking back is usually not knowledge but practice: finding words fast, saying sounds clearly, and feeling relaxed. So both plans spend most of the time on your voice: shadowing, recording yourself, talking with AI and real people.

The General plan builds the English of daily life: small talk, appointments, shopping, health, travel, stories and opinions.

The Quran plan uses the material you already love: stories of the prophets told in English, tajweed explained in English, and the meanings of the surahs you teach. About a third of the time (live partner, AI chats) stays on everyday English, so daily life improves too.

You also have a rare advantage. Tajweed trained your ear and mouth to notice exact articulation points (مخارج). Both plans use that skill for English sounds.

Why this works

  • A balanced diet of English. Paul Nation's "four strands" model gives roughly equal time to listening, speaking, studying phrases and fluency practice. The daily routine follows that balance.
  • Shadowing (speaking along with a recording) improves listening and the rhythm of speech.
  • Repeating the same task makes speech smoother and more accurate each time. That is why you record, listen and record again, and why the 4/3/2 drill repeats one talk three times.
  • Spaced flashcards are one of the strongest findings in learning research: reviewing just before you forget makes words stick.
  • Clear before perfect. Research on being understood puts consonants, vowel length and word stress first, so the sounds come in that order.

Realistic expectations. Cambridge estimates about 200 hours of guided learning to move up one level. These 12 weeks give about 60 focused hours: enough for a clear jump in confidence, fluency and pronunciation. Reaching a full C1 usually takes 6–12 months, so after week 12 you repeat the cycle with harder material.

Week 0 · one evening

Set up and record your starting point

Every day · about 50 minutes, plus easy listening

Daily routine

Blocks 1 and 3 are the same every day. Block 2 changes by day (see the weekly rhythm). The easy listening fits into time you already have.

15 min

Listen and shadow

Play 1–2 minutes of a 6 Minute English episode or a British Council lesson (both have transcripts). Listen once, read along once, then speak with the speaker, copying their rhythm and melody. Repeat 3–4 times.

Play 1–2 minutes of this week's prophet story (Mufti Menk's series, or a Yaqeen Institute video with English captions on). Listen once, read the captions once, then speak with the speaker, copying rhythm and melody. Repeat 3–4 times.

25 min

Today's speaking focus

The day's task from the weekly rhythm. Always out loud, and usually recorded.

10 min

Phrases and review

Add 5 useful phrases from today to your notebook and Anki, plus the correct version of any mistake you made. Review old cards. Say each phrase aloud in a full sentence, not just the word.

Add 5 phrases from today to Anki, the correct version of any mistake you made, and the English meaning of 2–3 verses from a surah you teach (from Quran.com). Say each one aloud in a full sentence.

20–30 min

Easy listening, anytime

While cooking, walking or travelling, listen to something you enjoy and understand without effort: older 6 Minute English episodes, Luke's English Podcast, TED-Ed. No notes, no pressure. This is where new words quietly come from.

While cooking, walking or travelling, listen to something you enjoy and understand without effort: full episodes of Mufti Menk's Stories of the Prophets or a Yaqeen Institute talk. No notes, no pressure. This is where new words quietly come from.

The two rules that keep you going. On a busy or tired day, do the 10-minute minimum: one shadowing round and one recording. It still counts. And never miss two days in a row: one missed day is normal; two in a row is how plans stop.

All day, free: think in English while cooking or walking. Describe what you are doing in your head ("I'm going to call the clinic and book an appointment")("I'm going to prepare tomorrow's lesson on madd"). When a word is missing, note it and look it up later.

Pronunciation for Arabic speakers · both plans

Use your tajweed ear

You already know where each Arabic letter comes from in the mouth. Many English sounds map neatly onto that knowledge.

English soundArabic bridgePractise with
/θ/ think, /ð/ thisSame place as ث and ذ. You already have these sounds; just use them.think / sink, three / tree, this / dis
/p/ vs /b//p/ is ب with no voice and a puff of air. Hold your hand in front of your lips: you should feel the puff on /p/ only.pen / Ben, park / bark, pray / bray
/v/ vs /f//f/ is ف. /v/ is the same mouth position with the voice on (feel your throat buzz).fan / van, fine / vine, safe / save
Short and long vowelsArabic has 3 vowel qualities; English has about 12. Length alone is not enough: the mouth shape changes too.ship / sheep, full / fool, cat / cut / cot, bed / bad
Consonant clustersEnglish allows several consonants together. Do not add a vowel before or between them.street (not "estreet"), spring, months, asked
/tʃ/ vs /ʃ//ʃ/ is ش. /tʃ/ starts with a short /t/ stop.chair / share, watch / wash, cheap / sheep
/ŋ/ singLike the nasal sound in ikhfa' before ك: the back of the tongue closes, with no /g/ after it.sing, long, morning, teaching
English rNot the tapped ر. The tongue curls back and does not touch the roof of the mouth.read, very, correct, Quran
Word stressLike madd, length and strength matter. One syllable in each word is longer and louder.TEAcher, eDUcation, PRACtise, underSTAND

Check any word's stress and sound in the Cambridge Dictionary (UK and US audio), and hear it in real sentences on YouGlish. The aim is to be clear, not to copy one accent; British and American sources are both fine.

Everyday English

Daily-life phrases

Natural phrases for the situations in the 12-week map. Say each one aloud, then change it to fit your own life.

Small talk

  • How's your week been so far?
  • I've been really busy lately, to be honest.
  • What do you do in your free time?
  • Oh, that sounds lovely!
  • Sorry, I didn't catch that. Could you say it again?

Appointments and calls

  • Hi, I'd like to book an appointment, please.
  • Do you have anything available on Tuesday morning?
  • Could I speak to someone about my order?
  • Could you spell that for me, please?
  • I'll call back later. Thanks for your help.

Shops and problems

  • Excuse me, do you have this in a smaller size?
  • I bought this last week, but it doesn't work.
  • Would it be possible to get a refund?
  • I'm afraid there's been a mistake with the bill.

Opinions

  • Personally, I think…
  • I see your point, but…
  • It depends on the situation, really.
  • The main reason is that…
  • I hadn't thought of it that way.

In class

  • Let's begin with Al-Fatiha.
  • Listen carefully, then repeat after me.
  • Your turn. Take your time.
  • Stretch this sound for two counts.
  • Can you hear the difference?
  • Put your finger on the letter.
  • Close your lips gently on this one.
  • Almost! Let's try that again, slowly.
  • Excellent, masha'Allah! That was really clear.
  • Who can tell me what this sign means?

With parents

  • She's making great progress with her letters.
  • He's finding the long vowels a bit tricky at the moment.
  • Could you listen to him read for ten minutes each day?
  • Next week we'll focus on the rules of noon sakinah.
  • She's ready to start memorising Surat An-Naba'.
  • Please don't worry; this is very normal at this stage.
  • Do you have any questions about her progress?
  • Thank you for your support at home. It really shows.

Telling a story

  • A long, long time ago, Allah sent a prophet called…
  • His people didn't want to listen to him.
  • But he never gave up. He was patient.
  • Then something amazing happened…
  • What do you think he did next?
  • So what can we learn from this story?

Speaking practice · both plans

Three ways to get speaking time

1 · Record yourself

The most important habit. Speak for 1–2 minutes, listen back, note 3 things to improve, then record again. Any mistake you hear more than once goes on your top-mistakes list. Compare recordings month by month.

2 · Talk with people, twice a week

Real conversation is where speaking grows fastest: 30 minutes on Wednesday and 15 minutes on Monday. Language exchange on Tandem or HelloTalk (free tiers): you teach Arabic, they help with English. Or join a free online study circle held in English.

3 · Talk with AI

Free voice chat in the ChatGPT or Gemini app, as extra practice or when no one is free. Free tiers have daily limits, so 15 minutes is the right size. Use the prompt below.

AI speaking partner prompt

You are my English speaking partner. I am a B2 learner and I want to speak more fluently in everyday life. Today we will talk about: [TOPIC].You are my English speaking partner. I am a B2 learner and I teach Quran to young children who don't speak Arabic. Today we will talk about: [TOPIC]. Rules: - Keep each of your turns short (2–3 sentences) and always end with a question, so I do most of the talking. - Speak naturally, at normal speed. - After every 5 of my answers, stop and give me: 3 mistakes I made with the correct version, 2 more natural ways to say something I said, and 1 useful phrase for my topic. - If I pause for a long time, help me with the first words of a sentence.

Topic ideas: your neighbourhood · a meal you cook well · a problem with a delivery · a trip you remember · your dream holiday · is social media good for children? · how life has changed since you were young.

Topic ideas: this week's prophet story · explain what tajweed is to a curious neighbour · a parent says their child is bored in class · your best and hardest teaching day · plan a lesson on qalqalah · should children learn Quran online or in person?

Staying safe with strangers online: choose female partners, keep chats inside the app, don't share your phone number, address or photos, and leave any chat that feels uncomfortable. A sister, friend or colleague who speaks English is always a good first partner.
